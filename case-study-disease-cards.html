CLINICAL UX · PRODUCT REDESIGN

Making clinical trial information easier to scan and prioritize.

I redesigned the Disease View to help clinical users quickly scan disease categories and identify relevant trial information when matching patients to clinical trials.

My Role

UX / Product Designer

Focus

Information hierarchy · Interaction design · UI design

Environment

Healthcare · Clinical trial technology

01 · THE PROBLEM

Dense, uneven cards made information difficult to scan.

The original cards changed height based on their content and presented conditions, biomarkers, and lines of therapy in dense blocks of text.

This created an inconsistent layout that made it harder to quickly compare disease areas and find relevant information.

GOAL

Create a more consistent, scannable experience without removing important clinical information.

BEFORE

Large, uneven cards created an inconsistent scanning pattern.

02 · THE REDESIGN

Creating a predictable structure across every card.

I reorganized each card around three clearly defined information groups:

Conditions · Biomarkers · Line of Therapy

01

Consistent card structure and height

02

Clearer information hierarchy

03

Progressive disclosure for additional information

04

Favoriting to keep important disease cards at the top

AFTER

The redesigned cards make information easier to scan, compare, and prioritize.

03 · KEY INTERACTION

Favorite the disease areas that matter most.

Users can favorite a disease card to keep it at the top of the page.

This reduces the need to repeatedly search through the full Disease View and keeps frequently referenced or currently relevant disease areas easy to access.

♡

FAVORITE & PRIORITIZE

Bookmarking gives users a lightweight way to personalize the Disease View around the disease areas they reference most.

OUTCOME

A clearer, more scalable Disease View.

The redesign transformed an uneven, text-heavy interface into a consistent card system that makes complex clinical trial information easier to navigate and prioritize.
