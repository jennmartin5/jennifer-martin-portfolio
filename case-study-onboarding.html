PRODUCT ONBOARDING · UX STRATEGY

Designing a more scalable way to onboard users.

I designed a role-based onboarding experience for a complex clinical trial match search product, giving users a guided path to the training and workflows most relevant to their responsibilities.

My Role

Product / UX Designer

Focus

UX strategy · Information architecture · User flows · Interaction design · Prototyping

Environment

Healthcare · Clinical research technology

Status

Designed · Awaiting engineering scope

01 · THE PROBLEM

The existing training model wasn't scalable.

Our product supports complex clinical research workflows that require meaningful onboarding and training.

Much of that training currently depends on a single Product Manager who leads both virtual and in-person sessions, often for large groups of users. As training needs have grown, this has created an unsustainable burden and contributed to burnout.

The team already maintains videos, PDFs, and supporting resources in SharePoint, but those self-service materials have not received enough engagement to meaningfully reduce reliance on instructor-led training.

I saw an opportunity to address both problems: make training easier for users to discover and complete while creating a more scalable model that reduces dependency on a single trainer.

DESIGN QUESTION

How might we create a scalable, self-guided onboarding experience that gives users the right training for their role while reducing reliance on instructor-led training?

02 · MY APPROACH

I reframed training as part of the product experience.

Instead of asking users to find the right training, I designed a system that brings the right training to them.

Different users have different responsibilities and workflows, so a single onboarding sequence would introduce unnecessary information. I structured the experience around role-based pathways instead.

Each pathway prioritizes the resources and product guidance a user needs to get started, while keeping additional learning available as it becomes relevant.

01

Role-based

Surface training based on the user's responsibilities instead of presenting the entire resource library.

02

Contextual

Bring guidance closer to the workflow so users do not have to leave the product to find help.

03

Progressive

Introduce essential information first and deeper learning as users continue through the product.

04

Scalable

Support additional roles, resources, and workflows without rebuilding the onboarding experience.

03 · DESIGN PROCESS

From an operational problem to a product solution.

01

Understand the current experience

I examined how training was currently delivered and where the experience created friction for both users and the team. Users had to leave the product, navigate to SharePoint, locate the right material, and determine which resources applied to their responsibilities.

02

Define the onboarding model

I organized the experience around user roles and mapped which resources and product activities should be prioritized for each pathway. This allowed onboarding to become more targeted without duplicating the underlying training library.

03

Design the experience

I designed a Getting Started checklist as the primary onboarding mechanism, supported by training resources, progress visibility, and contextual product guidance.

04

Plan for delivery

I considered implementation constraints alongside the experience itself. The native solution requires engineering capacity, so I began investigating an interim approach that could address the training need sooner without changing the long-term product direction.

04 · THE SOLUTION

A guided starting point tailored to each user.

The role-based onboarding experience gives users a clear path through training while keeping learning connected to the product.

01

Clear starting point

New users receive a defined onboarding path instead of having to discover training independently.

02

Role-based pathways

Users see the training and resources most relevant to their responsibilities rather than the entire library.

03

Visible progress

A checklist creates a clear sequence and helps users understand what they have completed and what remains.

05 · DELIVERY STRATEGY

Exploring an interim path to deliver value sooner.

The need for a more scalable onboarding model is established. The remaining constraint is engineering capacity.

The role-based onboarding experience represents the intended long-term product direction. Building the experience natively requires engineering capacity and will need to be brought into scope alongside other product priorities.

Rather than waiting for that capacity before addressing the training burden, I am researching Userflow as an interim solution. I am evaluating how effectively it can deliver the core onboarding experience within the existing product with significantly less engineering effort.

As part of that research, I am building a working proof of concept to test the behaviors that matter most and determine how closely Userflow can support the intended experience.

USERFLOW EVALUATION

New-user onboarding · Role-based checklists · Training resources · Contextual tooltips · Guided walkthroughs · Progress tracking · Restart and reset behavior

If the evaluation demonstrates that Userflow can support the experience effectively, it provides a path to begin reducing the current training burden sooner while the native solution waits to be brought into engineering scope.

What we learn from the interim experience can also inform requirements, user behavior, and implementation decisions when the full native solution moves into development.

06 · SUCCESS CRITERIA

Measuring whether onboarding actually reduces the training burden.

Success is not simply whether users open the onboarding experience. The larger goal is to determine whether self-guided onboarding helps users become more independent while reducing reliance on instructor-led training.

Onboarding completion

Track completion of priority items within each role-based pathway.

Training engagement

Measure whether users interact more consistently with self-service resources when they are surfaced in context.

User independence

Evaluate whether users can reach important workflows with less direct assistance.

Training demand

Monitor whether repeated questions and dependence on instructor-led sessions decrease over time.

07 · OUTCOME

A scalable direction ready to move forward when capacity allows.

What began as a question about increasing engagement with SharePoint became a broader strategy for reducing training dependency through product-led onboarding.

The native role-based experience establishes the long-term product direction, while the Userflow research provides a potential interim path for addressing the immediate training need.

The next step is to determine whether Userflow can effectively support the experience in the near term while the native solution waits to be prioritized into engineering scope.

THE STRATEGIC SHIFT

From: “How do we get users to visit our training site?”

↓

To: “How do we make onboarding more scalable and bring the right learning to the user?”

REFLECTION

The solution needed to work for users and for the team supporting them.

This project reinforced that a product experience can solve more than an interface problem.

By looking beyond the original request to increase engagement with training resources, I identified an opportunity to address a larger operational challenge: creating a more sustainable way to onboard users without placing the responsibility primarily on one person.

It also required balancing the ideal long-term experience with the realities of product prioritization and engineering capacity. Researching an interim delivery approach allows the team to continue moving toward that goal while preserving the native experience as the long-term direction.
