0→1 PRODUCT DESIGN · HEALTHCARE

Designing a platform for end-to-end clinical data integration.

I designed Connect, Genospace's platform for centralizing the configuration, mapping, monitoring, and transfer of clinical study data from EHR sources into external EDC systems.

My Role

Product / UX Designer

Focus

0→1 product design · User research · Complex workflows · Interaction design · Prototyping

Users

Clinical data integration teams · Administrators

Timeline

2023–Present

Status

Ongoing · Delivered in waves

THE PRODUCT

One platform for a highly complex integration workflow.

Connect is Genospace's internal platform for managing the end-to-end integration of clinical study data from Genospace and EHR sources into external EDC systems used by sponsors and research partners.

Behind that transfer is a complex operational workflow involving study configuration, subjects and visits, clinical data mapping, external system connections, transmission monitoring, and error resolution.

My work has focused on translating those highly technical workflows into a product experience the integration team can manage from one place.

THE PROBLEM

Clinical data moved through manual work and fragmented systems.

01

At the clinical site

Clinical research coordinators and data-entry teams manually reviewed patient charts in the EHR and re-entered relevant labs, vitals, and visit observations into a sponsor's EDC system.

The process could take multiple days and introduced opportunities for transcription errors and additional query-resolution work.

02

Inside the integration team

The automated integration workflow itself was spread across legacy CTMS screens, spreadsheets, configuration files, backend logs, work queues, and status fields.

Users had to move between systems to configure studies, monitor transfers, and determine what went wrong when data was missing or a transfer failed.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

How might we turn a fragmented and highly technical integration process into a centralized product experience that supports the way the team actually works?

MY ROLE

The product vision existed. The experience didn't.

I joined the initiative at its earliest stage. The Product Manager and data integration team understood the capabilities they needed, but the workflows and interface for Connect had not yet been defined.

Clinical trials and data integration were completely new domains for me, so my first job was learning how the team actually worked. I participated in ongoing workflow discussions, shadowed integration users, and worked closely with the Product Manager to understand both the clinical context and the technical processes behind the product.

I translated those conversations into early wireframes and concepts. Those designs gave the team something concrete to react to — helping us uncover requirements, question assumptions, remove unnecessary elements, and progressively define what Connect needed to become.

01 · BUILDING THE FOUNDATION

Starting with study configuration.

My earliest Connect designs focused on Study Management. Before data could be mapped or transferred, administrators needed a centralized way to configure the ecosystem surrounding a clinical study.

I designed an administrative experience for managing the connections, sponsors, sites, and other configuration required to begin an integration. These early screens also became working artifacts for defining how the new product should behave.

02 · CRF MAPPING

Deliver the capability first. Evolve the experience next.

CRF mapping became one of the longest and most complex workflows in Connect. The integration team needed to map Genospace source data to the corresponding fields required by an external EDC.

Building the complete mapping interface would take time. Rather than making users wait for the final experience, I introduced an interim JSON editor directly within the study's CRF Mapping area.

This brought the capability into Connect earlier while I continued working closely with users and the Product Manager to understand the workflow and define a more scalable interface.

WAVE 1

Bring the workflow into Connect.

The interim JSON editor allowed the team to manage mapping configuration inside the product before the complete interface was available.

WAVE 2

Turn technical configuration into a product experience.

As our understanding matured, I translated the underlying configuration into a structured mapping table that exposes the information and actions users need without requiring them to work directly in raw JSON.

The experience supports the team's mapping workflow through structured fields, query generation, values, publishing controls, and other study-level actions.

DESIGNING WITH DOMAIN EXPERTS

The hardest part wasn't designing a table. It was learning a highly specialized workflow well enough to improve it. I built close working relationships with integration users, continuously asked questions, observed how they worked, and treated them as ongoing design partners throughout the evolution of the experience.

03 · STUDY SURVEILLANCE

Moving from patient-by-patient monitoring to one study-level view.

Another fragmented workflow lived in the legacy CTMS. Integration users had to navigate patient by patient through individual schedule matrices to review visit dates, confirm event triggers, and investigate potential issues.

I designed a new Subjects experience inside each study that brings active EHR→EDC subjects and their visit schedules together in a centralized surveillance table.

The design combines visit and schedule information with missing-data signals and work-queue errors so users can identify events requiring attention without manually inspecting every patient.

HOW WE BUILD

A 0→1 product delivered in waves.

Connect has never been a single design-and-launch project. Its workflows are highly interconnected and technically complex, so the product has continued to evolve through incremental releases and new capabilities.

That has required balancing the long-term experience with what can realistically be delivered now. In some cases, like CRF Mapping, an interim solution provided value while the more complete experience was being defined and built.

This approach has also allowed our understanding of the product to mature alongside the product itself.

WHERE IT IS TODAY

The product is still evolving.

IN USE

Study configuration & CRF Mapping

Earlier Connect capabilities have progressed into the product, including the structured CRF Mapping experience used by the integration team today.

DESIGNED

Subjects surveillance

My most recent major design consolidates subject and visit surveillance at the study level and is awaiting engineering implementation.

EXPLORING

AI-assisted mapping

The team is beginning early research into how AI could support the effort involved in building CRF mappings. This work is exploratory and has not yet moved into product design.

REFLECTION

Designing when I'm not the domain expert.

When I joined this project, I was new to clinical trials and unfamiliar with the highly specialized work performed by the integration team. Learning that domain became a fundamental part of my design process.

Over time, I learned that my job wasn't to arrive with all the answers. It was to build enough trust with the people who understood the work to keep asking questions, make their knowledge visible through design, and create something we could evaluate together.

Connect has reinforced how I approach complex 0→1 work: understand deeply, give ideas form early, learn through collaboration, and continue evolving the product as both the users' needs and our understanding grow.
